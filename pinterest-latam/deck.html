The thesis

LATAM's monetization gap is a supply problem before it is a sales problem.

My job is to manufacture local shoppable inventory in Brazil and Hispanic LATAM — using demand data no competitor has, without paying creators a cent, and to prove it in revenue terms.

Beat 1

Supply is the constraint, not demand

80 billion monthly searches, 96% unbranded, more than half commercially intended — Bill Ready's figure, and it is global. Pinterest publishes no LATAM search breakout, so I'd say that rather than imply one. What is observable per market is Pinterest Trends, live in Portuguese with two years of history — the queries are visible country by country. The content answering them isn't there.

Beat 2

Pinterest can't buy its way out

Policy forbids paying per Pin. There is no creator fund. The only levers are education, tooling, status, distribution and partnership.

Beat 3

The competitive answer isn't GMV, it's access

Named, not vague: TikTok Shop Brazil pays 10–30% on a 14-day first-click window. Mercado Libre pays up to 16% in Brazil, up to 24% in Mexico and Chile, plus payment for video views at its Creador tier. Shopee pays 3% on external social traffic. Beat 4

Measure on 12-month cohorts

Pins peak in year two. A function measured on 30-day lift looks like a failure and dies in its first budget cycle.

This is supply-side growth marketing, aimed at creators, in a region with enormous reach and almost no revenue.

Job 1 — acquire

Get more small and mid-sized creators publishing

Across Brazil and Hispanic LATAM, at a scale that hand-recruitment cannot reach.

Job 2 — activate

Drive adoption of the features that make content commercially useful

Product tags, destination links, the paid-partnership tool, Amazon storefront linking, catalog feeds.

Job 3 — scale

Run the education, incentives and events that make both happen programmatically

At scale, rather than one relationship at a time.

"Content supplier" is not recruiter language.

It is genuinely how Pinterest's Global Content Organization describes creators internally — and it tells you the mental model. Creators are an input to the graph, not a partner class to be monetized. The org's own careers page says its job is to "source, foster and lighthouse content."

The causal chain the role sits at the front of
creators publish → good local content exists → shoppers search and click → advertisers see conversions → advertisers bid more per click → Pinterest's revenue goes up. Creators sit at the very front of that chain and touch none of the money.

Strategic Partner Manager vs Community Development

  • SPM — high-touch, vertical-specific, 1:1 with individual creators and publishers.
  • Community Development (this role) — the scaled, programmatic, data-driven layer beside them: email, in-product notifications, ads to creators, incentive mechanics, events, and the analytics that prove it moved content volume and quality.

The timing fact that should shape everything

Earnings peak in years three to six

Among creators with five-plus years, half clear R$10k/month. YOUPIX's own summary: "revenue grows over time, there's no miracle."

Consequence: the creator you activate today is worth most in 2029. That is an argument for 12-month cohort measurement, and against judging this function on 30-day lift.

Demographics — unusual, and they matter

~75% women; ~50% Black or Afro-Brazilian

  • Consistent across Reglab, Squid and YOUPIX. YOUPIX 2025 found 49.8% identify as Black or Afro-Brazilian, with a documented and persistent earnings gap versus white creators.
  • 74.8% have experienced hate speech. Mental health is a standing agenda item in Brazilian creator programmes, not a footnote.
  • Pinterest's own audience skews ~70% female — the demographic fit is unusually good.
Formalisation is an income lever

PJ creators earn roughly double

  • R$10,115 vs R$5,078/month — legal entity versus individual (FGV study).
  • MEI is the common entry vehicle but its revenue ceiling pushes successful creators toward full CNPJ. Nota fiscal and income-tax treatment are live 2026 topics.
  • Mexico's 54.8% labour informality means formalisation there is far weaker — a different curriculum module.

The economics that explain retention

A mid-tier Brazilian creator (100k–500k) earns R$3,000–15,000 per sponsored campaign — lumpy, negotiated, capped by how many brand deals exist. Reframe 1

"Afiliado" means something different in Brazil

To a Brazilian creator the word primarily evokes Hotmart and the infoproduct economy — courses, ebooks, digital products at 30–80% commission with 60–180 day cookies. That is an order of magnitude more money per conversion than any retail affiliate programme. Retail affiliate is the second meaning. Any conversation about creator monetization in Brazil that ignores this ecosystem is missing the biggest piece.

An FGV study commissioned by Hotmart put average monthly income at R$11,959 for creators whose digital sales are their primary income. Small sample, vendor-commissioned — treat directionally.

Reframe 2 — and this is the opening

Shopee is now paying creators double to drive traffic from outside its own app

On 1 July 2026 Shopee halved the Shopee Video commission from 3% to 1.5% while keeping external social links at 3%. Which means Shopee is actively paying its five million affiliates more to bring traffic in from outside than from inside.

Pinterest is precisely that outside channel — and Shopee's own affiliate terms, clause 2.3(a), already name Pinterest as an approved channel, while 2.3(b) permits Pinterest Ads and bans Google Ads and Bing Ads. The timing to make that argument is right now.

Where the affiliate channel skews
YOUPIX finds affiliate commissions are now the #2 income source for Brazilian creators, up from #3 in 2022, behind sponsored posts — and that affiliate revenue peaks in a creator's first one to two years and declines after five. So the affiliate channel skews toward emerging creators, which maps exactly onto the JD's "small and medium content suppliers."

Brazil · Self-regulation

CONAR's new influencer guide — 13 May 2026, effective immediately

Replaces the 2021 version. It establishes three content categories:

  • Publicidade — paid.
  • "Mensagem Ativada" — the new one. Non-paid benefits like gifts and trips, which now require disclosure.
  • Conteúdo Orgânico.

Disclosure must use the platform's own paid-partnership tool, or an ostensive #publicidade / #publi visible without interaction. Crucially it pushes responsibility onto advertisers and agencies, who must brief creators, monitor content and correct violations. AI content is subject to the same ethical rules but there is still no AI-disclosure requirement.

CONAR is self-regulation, not law. Say so — people fumble that distinction.

Brazil · Binding law — the bigger deal

ECA Digital (Lei 15.211/2025)

  • Compliance deadline was 17 March 2026. Final guidance lands August 2026; administrative sanctions begin November 2026.
  • Requires reliable age verification, guardian-linked accounts for under-16s, privacy by default, and prohibits profiling minors for advertising.
  • For this ecosystem specifically: monetising child influencers now requires prior judicial authorisation.
  • Enforcement sits with the ANPD. Penalties reach 10% of Brazilian group revenue.
  • Any programme touching under-18 creators needs legal review.
Colombia

Tax compliance, mandatory disclosure, and consumer-protection classification with administrative sanctions. Formalisation pressure is real and rising.

03 · The holistic approach

Feature adoption, ordered by proximity to revenue.

"Drive feature adoption" is the JD's explicit goal. This is the ladder, and the order is not arbitrary — it runs from housekeeping to the one rung with a direct line to Pinterest's revenue.

The trap that belongs on page one of any monetisation curriculum

An affiliate link permanently disqualifies a Pin from ever being promoted

Pinterest's own wording: "Pins that contain mentions and/or affiliate links are currently not eligible for promotion." So a creator has to decide before publishing whether a piece of content is an affiliate play or a brand play. It cannot be both. That is a genuinely non-obvious rule with real money attached.

The operational failure point nobody documents

Creation is mobile-only. Approval is desktop-only.

Creator side: Create a Pin → Advanced settings → "Add paid partnership label" → "Add a brand partner" → search and select. One brand per Pin. The label publishes immediately without the brand name; the name appears once they approve. Brand side: "At this time, you can only approve paid partnership tags from your desktop device."

Creators can't tag from desktop, brands can't approve from a phone, and approval delays are structurally baked in. Also: removal is irreversible — once a brand removes a tag it cannot be re-added, and any live ad using it goes straight to Archive.

LATAM coverage: six of the ~19 original 2021 launch markets were Latin American — Brazil, Mexico, Argentina, Chile, Colombia and Peru. In Portuguese the on-Pin label is "Parceria paga." Pinterest publishes no current authoritative country list, so verify per market before building a programme around it.

03 · The holistic approach

Six loops that compound — and one to guard against.

Loop 1

Demand → supply → monetisation

Gap brief identifies underserved queries → creators fill them → search fulfilment rises → sessions lengthen → more monetisable impressions → advertiser results improve → better proof → more creators.

Loop 2 — why the proof library is the first deliverable

Proof → recruitment

Every creator who earns becomes a case study → case studies recruit the next cohort → acquisition gets cheaper every cycle. Without this, you are recruiting against TikTok Shop with abstractions.

Loop 3

Channel compounding

One partnership with Shopee's affiliate team reaches more creators than a year of 1:1 outreach → those creators' success becomes the argument for the next partner (Hotmart, Mercado Livre, Magalu) → each partnership makes the next easier to sign.

Loop 4

Certification → status → advocacy

Certified creators teach other creators → community-led education scales without headcount → the certification becomes worth more as more people hold it.

Loop 5 — the budget-justifying one

Merchant → advertiser

Creator-merchants and infoproduct creators who see organic results buy ads → direct revenue attributable to the content org → budget justification → more programme capacity.

Loop 6

Content → data → ranking → content

More local content → richer Taste Graph for Brazilian and Spanish-language taste → better recommendations → higher engagement → better ranking signal → the platform itself gets better at distributing local content.

The anti-loop — design against it from day one
Volume incentives → spam → feed quality drops → users disengage → advertisers leave. Pinterest's policy forbids paying per Pin precisely because of this: "incentivize or pay people to distribute content in large volumes, in spammy ways or for each Pin they save" is explicitly prohibited. Every incentive must be quality-gated. The affiliate segment is where this risk concentrates.
Why the loops matter to the pricing problem
Better content also sharpens the taste signal, which improves targeting, which improves ad pricing — and Pinterest's specific problem is impressions up 41% with pricing down 19%. Supply quality is not just a volume story; it's a yield story.

04 · Growth strategy · Initiative 03

A 20× throughput difference sits behind two undocumented features.

What creators actually use

The native scheduler is deliberately crippled

10 Pins in the queue, 30 days out, one at a time. This is what every Brazilian creator knows about, and it's the ceiling they think they're working under.

Path: Settings → "Create Pins in bulk" → Auto-publish → Connect RSS feed

RSS auto-publish — 200 Pins a day, free, forever

  • Buried under Settings, not under Create. That is why nobody finds it. Desktop only.
  • "You can add as many RSS feeds as you want, as long as they match your claimed website."
  • Limit of 200 Pins per day; new items appear within 24 hours.
  • Title and description come from <title> and <description>; images from <image>, <enclosure> and <media:content> under each <item>.
  • One board per feed — so the pattern to teach is multiple category feeds → multiple boards, which is explicitly allowed and turns a WordPress blog into a 200-image-a-day publishing machine with zero ongoing effort.
  • Feeds process oldest first. RSS 2.* and 1.* (RDF) supported; Atom is not. Images only — video does not work through RSS.
help.pinterest.com/business/article/bulk-upload-video-pins

CSV bulk upload — 200 per submission

  • Up to 200 images or videos per submission, 200 videos/day, desktop only.
  • An optional publish date that bypasses the native scheduler's 10-Pin / 30-day ceiling entirely.
  • Required columns: Title (100 char), Media URL (public link ending .mp4/.png/.jpg), Board, and Thumbnail for video.
  • Optional: Description (500 char), Link, Publish date, Keywords. No alt-text column.

04 · Growth strategy · The education spine

The curriculum — and the two corrections that make it credible.

Education is the largest single workstream and the one with the clearest deliverables. But most Pinterest SEO teaching in circulation is folklore, and a LATAM education programme built on folklore is a liability.

Correction 1 · The second-most-valuable text signal, and almost nobody teaches it

Board titles are a first-class ranking input — with published numbers

Pinterest's OmniSearchSage paper publishes an ablation table showing the incremental gain from each feature group:

Feature addedSaveLong-clickRelevance
Title + description + synthetic captions+21%+19%+30%
Board titles+17%+8%+13%
Engaged queries+7%+7%+5%

The deeper mechanism: PinSage is a graph network over the Pin–board graph, so a Pin's embedding is partly a function of what else sits on the boards it's saved to. Coherent boards produce coherent neighbourhoods; grab-bag boards produce diffuse ones. Pinterest's own guidance quietly agrees — "make sure your board title and description are specific and focused" and "aim for 20–100 Pins per board or per board section."

This is also the honest answer to "owning a niche." Pinterest has never published an account-level topical authority score. Teaching "Pinterest rewards niched accounts" as fact is unsupported. What is documented is board-level topical tightness, mechanically, with numbers. That version is both truer and more actionable.

Correction 2 · Every Pinterest SEO guide in circulation gets this wrong

Pin alt text is not an SEO field

Pinterest's own help doc: "Alternative text descriptions will only be read to people using a screen reader." It appears in no published indexed-field list.

The confusion is real and worth teaching precisely: Pinterest's Pin2Interest system does index "link alt text" — the alt attribute of images on your destination webpage, which Pinterest crawls. Different field entirely. So: write your website's alt text for both accessibility and indexing; write your Pin's alt text for accessibility only.

The mechanism that should anchor all creative training

The relevance model reads the image only through a text proxy

Per Pinterest's own engineering blog, the production cross-encoder judges a Pin against a query using five inputs: Pin title and description; synthetic image captions generated by BLIP; the top engaged queries for that Pin over the past two years; board titles where the Pin has been saved; and the title and description of the linked webpage. Even the May 2025 visual search overhaul works this way — the VLM turns the image into words, then the words drive retrieval.

So the practical creative rule is: your image must be legible to a captioning model, not just beautiful to a human. A single clear subject, well lit, unoccluded, in recognisable context, captions accurately and feeds a real ranking input. Abstract, collage-heavy or text-overlaid images caption generically — "a poster with text" — and silently degrade that input.

And: Pinterest has never published that on-image text overlay is OCR'd and indexed. "Put your keywords in the text overlay" is folklore. Drop it.

Two more things to teach precisely
  • Cadence. The only posting-cadence number Pinterest publishes is "aim to create original content weekly." Every "5 pins a day" or "15–25 pins a day" figure in circulation is unsourced. Teach the published number.
  • Saves are the optimisation objective, not clicks. Every Pinterest ranking paper reports repins/saves as the headline metric — TransActV2 +6.35% repins, OmniSage +2.5% sitewide repins, PinRec +4% search saves — with hides as an explicit negative (−12.8%). Teach save-worthiness. Clicks are the monetisation event but not the ranking objective.

05 · Measurement

Two method decisions get made in your first quarter, and they decide whether the function survives.

Decision 1 · The holdout

Insist on a control group before any programme launches

Creator programmes are notorious for claiming credit for people who would have published anyway. Running a proper control is unusual in this function — which is exactly why it works.

  • A holdout on every programme, not the flagship one.
  • It answers the question that actually matters: do incentivised creators publish more, or just publish the same amount with a prize attached? Almost nobody in creator marketing runs that test.
  • It is also the single decision that determines whether you can prove anything in month nine.

This is the line between a growth marketer and a community manager, and it's worth saying in exactly those terms.

Decision 2 · The measurement window

Fight for 12-month cohorts from day one

The product's value compounds over two years. Three documented facts make the case:

  • Engaged queries accrue over two years. A Pin's representation includes the top queries that drove engagement with it over the past 24 months — early click-through literally trains the Pin's future retrievability.
  • User memory runs to 16,000 actions over two years. Bill Ready, Q1 2026: "extending user context windows within search by 30-fold… up to 16,000 user actions over a 2-year period." Homefeed went 160×. A user who saved your content in 2024 remains reachable in 2026.
  • Over 60% of saves come from Pins more than a year old; performance peaks in year two.

A programme measured on 30-day lift will always look like a failure and will get cut. Push for 12-month cohort measurement from the start.

A third, quieter one

Language-split analysis

Portuguese versus Spanish content performance within the same categories — and whether es-LATAM content from Mexico travels to Argentina and Colombia. That single analysis determines whether you run one Hispanic LATAM programme or several, which is a headcount and budget question as much as a strategy one.

Say this Pins peak in year two. If we measure this function on thirty-day lift, it looks like a failure and gets cut. I'd rather have that argument in month one, when it's a measurement design decision, than in month nine, when it's a budget decision.

07 · Pinterest vs other channels

Advantages — each one with a mechanism, not a slogan.

The strongest line there is

Distribution without an audience

Query-driven, not follower-driven. TikTok Shop Mexico requires 1,000 followers. YouTube Shopping Brazil requires 5,000. Mercado Libre's Creador tier requires 10,000. Pinterest requires nothing — because a query summons your content regardless of who follows you. For the 78% of Brazilian creators under 50k followers, that isn't a feature; it's the only door that's open.

Nobody else can offer this

Demand intelligence, before you make anything

On TikTok you produce and hope. Here you can see demand first. No competitor has query logs to serve — they know what users watched, not what they were looking for. 80 billion searches a month, 96% unbranded.

Documented mechanism, not a claim

Content gets more retrievable over time, not less

A Pin's engaged queries accrue over two years and become part of how it ranks. Over 60% of saves come from Pins over a year old; performance peaks in year two — 68 saves versus 44 for fresher content. A TikTok has a 48-hour life. A Pin appreciates.

Two-year memory

The audience you earned stays reachable

Pinterest now uses up to 16,000 user actions over two years for context. Someone who saved your content in 2024 is still reachable in 2026.

A weakness for Pinterest, an arbitrage for the creator

Lowest competition per unit of demand in Brazil

42.2M ad reach versus Instagram's 147M, YouTube's 150M, TikTok's 131M. The searches exist; the content doesn't. Frame the small footprint honestly and it becomes the pitch.

Traffic quality

Pinterest catches people deciding, not scrolling

More than half of searches are commercial; 96% unbranded. Pinterest Assistant users show 25 percentage points higher commercial intent than traditional search. And even bloggers reporting declining Pinterest volume report it as among their highest-RPM traffic sources.

Throughput

Publishing capacity no other platform gives away

RSS auto-publish at 200 images a day, free and permanent; CSV bulk at 200 per submission against 10 in the native scheduler. Nothing comparable exists on Instagram or TikTok.

Risk profile

An annuity, not a lottery

TikTok has a huge jackpot and brutal variance. If you have no audience, variance kills you before the jackpot arrives. For 78% of the market, a compounding asset is the rational bet.

Demographic fit

The audience matches the supply

Pinterest LATAM skews 65–69% female; Brazilian creators are ~75% women; Colombian Pinterest users are 62% parents. The audience and the supply base are the same people — which is rarer than it sounds.

Two rules that keep you out of trouble, and the tier system behind every number in this deck.

Rule 1 · Say the scope before you say the number

Almost every impressive Pinterest statistic is global

Pinterest reports revenue and users by three geographies — US & Canada, Europe, Rest of World. It does not break out LATAM in its financials at all, and it publishes no LATAM search-volume figure. Every platform-behaviour statistic (searches, unbranded share, Gen Z share, gender split, save behaviour) is a global number.

So the discipline is: "Globally, Pinterest runs eighty billion searches a month, ninety-six percent of them unbranded. There's no published LATAM breakout — but Pinterest Trends works per country in Portuguese, so the demand signal is observable market by market even though the aggregate isn't public."

That sentence is more impressive than the raw stat, because it demonstrates you know the difference.

Rule 2 · Say what it measures before you say what it is

Most conflicts in this deck are definitional, not factual

Ad reach is not MAU. Creator counts depend entirely on the follower threshold. GMV share is not app usage. When two numbers disagree, it is almost always because they measure different things — and naming that is the sophisticated answer.

The four confidence tiers used throughout

Tier APinterest-published — 10-K, earnings calls, newsroom, help docs, engineering papers. Quote freely and attribute to Pinterest.
Tier BNamed third party — DataReportal, Nielsen, Influencity, YOUPIX, Squid, FGV, UBS BB, Sensor Tower, Tailwind. Quote with the source named out loud.
Tier CTrade press or vendor — single-outlet reporting, vendor marketing. Say "reported", not "is".
Tier DDerived or unverified — arithmetic, estimates, or claims with no primary source. Label them as such, or don't use them.
If you are asked directly where a number comes from That one's Bill Ready's, from the earnings calls — it's a global figure, and Pinterest doesn't publish a LATAM breakout.
